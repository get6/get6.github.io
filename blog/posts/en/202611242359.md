When you chase a goal, you make mistakes, you fail, and you get discouraged. This post is about **how to deal with failure** in those moments, told through the experience of taking a wrong turn on the road.

When mistakes and failures keep piling up, the goal starts to feel out of reach. It can feel like the whole thing is going to end in failure.

## Turning Back from a Wrong Road Isn't Going Backward

Here's an example. You're heading toward your destination, doing fine, and at some point you take a wrong road. You notice it, and you turn back.

Are you moving away from your destination? No. You're going back the way you came, but you're still **heading toward your destination**.

Every step you take turning back brings you closer. It may look like reversing, but it's actually **moving forward**.

## Don't Make the Failed Version of You Your Identity

That doesn't mean pretending the failure never happened. You can admit to yourself that you failed.

But don't put a **period** there. The moment you make the failed version of you who you are, the story ends right there.

Keep your mind on the **journey**. A mind that stays on the journey is one that can keep moving forward.

## Use a Comma Instead of a Period

Where you failed, try putting a **comma** instead of a period. A comma is just a place to catch your breath. The sentence keeps going.

And nobody decides for you whether it's a period or a comma. Choosing which one to put down is **something only you can do**.

## Three Things to Remember When You Fail

1. **Turning back is moving forward** — Noticing a wrong road and turning around brings you closer to where you're going.
2. **Failure isn't your identity** — You can admit you failed without making the failed version of you who you are.
3. **You place the comma** — Deciding to put a comma instead of a period is your own choice.
